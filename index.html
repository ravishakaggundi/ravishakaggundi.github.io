Data Analysis · EDA

FoodHub Order Analysis

Exploratory data analysis on 1,898 food delivery orders across 178 NYC restaurants. Answered 17 structured business questions to drive marketing, operations, and revenue strategy.

Net revenue$6,166
SLA breach10.54% orders >60 min
Top restaurantShake Shack $3,579
▼

Business Problem

  • FoodHub aggregates orders from multiple NYC restaurants via a single app and wants to understand demand patterns to improve customer experience and profitability
  • Dataset: 1,898 orders · 9 features · order_id, restaurant, cuisine, cost, day, rating, prep time, delivery time
  • Goal: Answer 17 business questions through EDA and recommend actionable improvements

EDA Approach

  • Univariate: Count plots for cuisine type and day-of-week distribution; histograms for order cost and delivery time
  • Bivariate: Boxplots of delivery time vs. rating; pointplots of cost vs. prep time by cuisine
  • Correlation heatmap: Numeric features to identify cost–prep time relationship
  • Revenue calc: Tiered commission model — 15% (≤$5), 20% ($5–20), 25% (>$20)

Key Findings

  • Top cuisines: American, Japanese, Italian dominate; Vietnamese and Spanish have lowest order share
  • Weekend effect: Significantly higher order volume; weekday mean delivery 28 min vs. weekend 22 min
  • Rating gap: 39% of orders left unrated — major feedback blind spot
  • Promo eligibility: The Meatball Shop (4.51), Blue Ribbon Fried Chicken (4.33), Shake Shack (4.28) qualify

Conclusions & Recommendations

  • 10.5% of orders exceed 60-min SLA; negative ratings spike above that threshold — prioritize logistics optimization
  • Launch weekday lunch combos and office-delivery discounts to close the weekday engagement gap
  • Loyalty program with personalized offers using order history to retain high-frequency customers
  • Share detailed feedback reports with partner restaurants to help reduce prep time

Computer Vision · Transfer Learning

HelmNet — Safety Helmet Detection

Binary image classifier for construction site PPE compliance on 631 images. Progressed from custom CNN to VGG-16 + data augmentation. Final model achieved 100% accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 — zero errors on test set.

Test Accuracy100%
Precision / Recall1.00 / 1.00
Inference~78ms/step
▼

Business Problem

  • Manual PPE compliance at construction/industrial sites misses violations in real time
  • Dataset: 631 images · 311 "With Helmet" · 320 "Without Helmet" · varied industrial environments
  • Small dataset → transfer learning from ImageNet essential

Model Progression

  • Model 1: Custom CNN from scratch — baseline
  • Model 2: VGG-16 frozen (ImageNet) — immediate improvement
  • Model 3: VGG-16 fine-tuned + FFNN head
  • Model 4: VGG-16 + augmentation (rotations, flips, shifts) → 100% accuracy

Results

  • Confusion matrix: 32 TP + 32 TN, zero errors
  • Augmentation's key value: robustness for real-world helmet orientations and lighting, not just current metrics

Conclusions

  • Export to TensorFlow Lite for edge deployment (Raspberry Pi, site entrance cameras)
  • 8-bit quantization → 75% model size reduction with no accuracy loss
  • Estimated ROI: 20–30% reduction in PPE violations at construction sites

SuperKart Sales Forecasting + Full Deployment

End-to-end ML pipeline — retail sales regression with Flask REST API, Streamlit UI, Docker container, deployed on HuggingFace Spaces. OUT004 generated highest revenue at 15.4M.

Top store revenue15,427,583 (OUT004)
DeploymentFlask + Streamlit on HuggingFace
▼

Business Problem

  • SuperKart needs quarterly sales forecasts per product-store to optimize inventory and supply chains
  • 4 stores (OUT001–OUT004) across Tier 1/2/3 cities and Supermarket/Grocery types

EDA Key Findings

  • OUT004 (Supermarket Type 2, Tier 2, medium) accounts for 53% of total revenue
  • Fruits & vegetables and snack foods are highest-selling categories across all stores

  • Tier 1 stores support higher-MRP products; Tier 3 stores benefit from high-volume, lower-MRP staples

NLP · RAG · Generative AI

Medical RAG Assistant

RAG-based Q&A over the 4,114-page Merck Manual using LangChain + ChromaDB + Mistral-7B. Baseline LLM hallucinated on all 5 clinical queries. RAG achieved perfect Groundedness (5/5) and Relevance (4–5/5) across all queries.

Groundedness5/5 all queries
Relevance4–5/5 all queries
Chunks indexed4,692 passages
▼

Business Problem

  • Merck Manual spans 4,114 pages across 23 sections — clinicians can't search it in real time
  • Baseline LLMs truncate at 128 tokens and hallucinate citations — clinically dangerous
  • Goal: Grounded Q&A with real Merck Manual citations, zero hallucinations

RAG Pipeline

  • PyMuPDFLoader → RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter (overlap=200) → 4,692 chunks
  • SentenceTransformerEmbeddings → ChromaDB (k=3 retrieval)
  • Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 (Q6_K quantized, llama.cpp) · temp=0

Evaluation Results

QueryGroundedRelevance
Sepsis management5/54/5
Appendicitis5/54/5
Alopecia areata5/55/5
TBI treatment5/54/5
Wilderness fracture5/54/5

Conclusions

  • Switch to BioBERT embeddings (+20% est. retrieval accuracy); increase max_tokens to 256–512
  • Expand k from 3→5–7 for multi-topic clinical queries
  • HIPAA/GDPR compliance required for any patient-facing deployment
