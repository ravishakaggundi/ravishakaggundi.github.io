FoodHub Order Analysis

Exploratory data analysis on 1,898 food delivery orders across 178 NYC restaurants. Answered 17 structured business questions to drive marketing, operations, and revenue strategy.

Net revenue$6,166
SLA breach10.54% orders >60 min
Top restaurantShake Shack $3,579
▼

Business Problem

  • FoodHub aggregates orders from multiple NYC restaurants via a single app and wants to understand demand patterns to improve customer experience and profitability
  • Dataset: 1,898 orders · 9 features · order_id, restaurant, cuisine, cost, day, rating, prep time, delivery time
  • Goal: Answer 17 business questions through EDA and recommend actionable improvements

EDA Approach

  • Univariate: Count plots for cuisine type and day-of-week distribution; histograms for order cost and delivery time
  • Bivariate: Boxplots of delivery time vs. rating; pointplots of cost vs. prep time by cuisine
  • Correlation heatmap: Numeric features to identify cost–prep time relationship
  • Revenue calc: Tiered commission model — 15% (≤$5), 20% ($5–20), 25% (>$20)

Key Findings

  • Top cuisines: American, Japanese, Italian dominate; Vietnamese and Spanish have lowest order share
  • Weekend effect: Significantly higher order volume; weekday mean delivery 28 min vs. weekend 22 min
  • Rating gap: 39% of orders left unrated — major feedback blind spot
  • Loyalty signal: Several customers placed 10+ orders — untapped loyalty program opportunity
  • Promo eligibility: The Meatball Shop (4.51), Blue Ribbon Fried Chicken (4.33), Shake Shack (4.28) qualify on rating + volume

Conclusions & Recommendations

  • Reduce high-delivery-time orders — 10.5% exceed 60-min SLA and negative ratings spike above that threshold
  • Launch weekday lunch combos and office-delivery discounts to close the weekday engagement gap
  • Loyalty program: discounts/freebies after N orders; personalized offers using order history
  • Run "Try Something New" campaigns for underordered cuisines (Vietnamese, Spanish)
  • Share detailed feedback reports with partner restaurants to help them optimize prep time

AllLife Bank Personal Loan Prediction

Predicted personal loan acceptance for 5,000 liability customers. Compared baseline, pre-pruned, and post-pruned Decision Trees. Best model (post-pruning, ccp_alpha=0.000641) achieved 98.4% accuracy and 91.8% F1.

Accuracy98.4%
F10.918
Recall0.906
▼

Business Problem

  • AllLife Bank wants to convert liability (deposit) customers into asset (loan) customers via targeted campaigns — without wasting budget on low-propensity customers
  • Dataset: 5,000 customers · 14 features · Age, Income, CCAvg, Education, Mortgage, Family, Securities/CD accounts
  • Class imbalance: Only 9.6% accepted the loan in the previous campaign

EDA Approach

  • Anomaly treatment: Negative Experience values identified and treated (data quality issue)
  • Bivariate analysis: Loan acceptance vs. income bracket — strong positive correlation above $98.5K
  • Education split: Grad/Professional (Level 2/3) show 10–20% higher acceptance than undergrads
  • CCAvg: Higher monthly credit card spend correlated strongly with loan uptake
  • CD account holders: 46.4% loan acceptance vs. 7.2% non-holders — strongest signal after income

Ensemble ML · SMOTE

EasyVisa Certification Prediction

US visa outcome prediction on 25,480 OFLC applications. Compared 6 ensemble classifiers × 3 sampling strategies. Gradient Boosting + SMOTE achieved Recall 0.871 and F1 0.826 on test set.

Test Recall0.871
Test F10.826
Test Accuracy75.6%
▼

Business Problem

  • OFLC processes 500K+ employer-sponsored visa applications/year. EasyVisa (consulting firm) needs a model to recommend Certified vs. Denied — enabling immigration lawyers and HR teams to prioritize strong candidates
  • Dataset: 25,480 applications · 12 features · continent, education, job_experience, prevailing_wage, unit_of_wage, full_time_position, region
  • Metric priority: Recall — missing a certifiable candidate is a direct revenue loss for the firm

EDA & Class Imbalance Strategy

  • Class split: 67% Certified / 33% Denied — moderate imbalance
  • Certification drivers: Doctorate (~87% certified), job experience (~74%), full-time roles, wages >$70K, Europe/Africa origin
  • Denial drivers: High School education (~34% certified), no experience, hourly wages, Island regions
  • 3 sampling strategies compared: Original · SMOTE oversampling · Random undersampling
  • SMOTE consistently outperformed on minority-class recall across all models

Bank Churn Prediction — Neural Networks

Predicted 6-month churn for 10,000 customers (20.4% churn rate). Compared 6 ANN variants across optimizer choice, dropout, and SMOTE. Final model: SMOTE + Adam + Dropout → validation recall 0.705.

Val Recall0.705
Test Accuracy77%
True Positives291 churners caught

Business Problem

  • Retaining a customer costs 5–25× less than acquiring one. Bank needs to flag churners 6 months out to enable proactive retention campaigns
  • Dataset: 10,000 customers · 14 features · CreditScore, Geography, Gender, Age, Tenure, Balance, NumOfProducts, IsActiveMember, EstimatedSalary
  • Class imbalance: 20.4% churned — SMOTE required on training set only (no leakage)
  • Metric priority: Recall — missing a churner is costlier than contacting a non-churner

EDA Key Findings

  • Inactive members: churn ~27% — nearly 2× active member rate
  • Age 40–50: highest-risk cohort; younger customers more loyal

  • Preprocessing: one-hot Geography, label-encode Gender, MinMax scale numerics, SMOTE on train set only

ReneWind Turbine Failure Prediction

Predictive maintenance on wind turbines using 40 anonymized sensor features (20K training samples, 5.55% failure rate). Model 5 — 256→128→64→32 ANN with Adam + Dropout — achieved 99.02% accuracy and 0.85 failure recall on test set.

Test Accuracy99.02%
Failure Recall0.85
Failure Precision0.98
▼

Business Problem

  • Undetected turbine generator failures cause catastrophic breakdowns — far more expensive than unnecessary maintenance checks
  • Asymmetric cost: False negative (missed failure) >> False positive (unnecessary inspection) → Recall is the primary metric
  • Dataset: 20K training + 5K test · 40 ciphered sensor features (V1–V40) · ~5.55% failure rate
  • Feature names anonymized — no domain interpretation possible; purely data-driven approach

Modeling Approach

  • Class imbalance strategy: Class weights via compute_class_weight (not SMOTE — sensor data continuity preserved)
  • Architecture search: 7 models (Model 0–6), progressively deeper with regularization
  • Added BatchNormalization for training stability on deep architectures
  • 50% Dropout to prevent co-adaptation in dense layers
  • Evaluated on recall-first basis — prioritized not missing failures over overall accuracy

HelmNet — Safety Helmet Detection

Binary image classifier for construction site PPE compliance — "With Helmet" vs "Without Helmet" on 631 images. Progressed from custom CNN to VGG-16 with data augmentation. Final model achieved 100% accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 on test set.

Test Accuracy100%
Precision / Recall / F11.00 / 1.00 / 1.00
Inference~78ms/step
▼

Business Problem

  • Manual PPE compliance checking at construction/industrial sites is labor-intensive and misses violations in real time
  • Dataset: 631 images · 311 "With Helmet" · 320 "Without Helmet" · varied environments (construction, factory, industrial)
  • Images resized to 200×200×3 RGB; loaded via Keras ImageDataGenerator with train/val/test split
  • Small dataset → transfer learning from ImageNet is essential

Model Progression

  • Model 1 — Custom CNN: Conv2D → MaxPool → Conv2D → MaxPool → Flatten → Dense → Sigmoid. Baseline from scratch
  • Model 2 — VGG-16 frozen: ImageNet weights, no top layers, GlobalAvgPool + Sigmoid head. Leverages low-level edge/texture features
  • Model 3 — VGG-16 fine-tuned: Unfroze upper VGG layers + Dense(256, ReLU) → Dropout → Sigmoid. Better domain adaptation
  • Model 4 — VGG-16 + Augmentation: Added rotations, flips, shifts, zoom. Decisive factor for real-world generalization

SuperKart Sales Forecasting + Full Deployment

End-to-end ML pipeline — retail sales regression with a Flask REST API backend, Streamlit UI frontend, Dockerized container, and full deployment on HuggingFace Spaces. OUT004 (Supermarket Type 2, Tier 2) generated the highest revenue at 15.4M.

Top store revenue15,427,583 (OUT004)
DeploymentFlask + Streamlit on HuggingFace
Models compared6 regressors
▼

Business Problem

  • SuperKart needs accurate quarterly sales forecasts per product-store combination to optimize inventory, reduce waste, and align supply chains
  • Target: Product_Store_Sales_Total (quarterly revenue per product per store)
  • Features: Product_Type, Product_MRP, Store_Size, Store_Location_City_Type, Store_Type, Store_Age_Years, Product_Fat_Content, Product_Visibility
  • 4 stores (OUT001–OUT004) across Tier 1/2/3 cities and Supermarket/Grocery types

EDA Key Findings

  • OUT004 (Supermarket Type 2, Tier 2, medium) — 53% of total revenue
  • OUT002 (Food Mart, Tier 3, small) — lowest revenue; different product mix needed
  • Fruits & vegetables and snack foods are highest-selling categories across all stores
  • OUT003 (Departmental, Tier 1) supports highest per-product price range ($3,070–$8,000)
  • Store type + city tier + product MRP are the strongest revenue predictors

Deployment Pipeline

  • Step 1: sklearn Pipeline — OneHotEncoder + numeric passthrough + regressor (consistent train/inference transforms)
  • Step 2: GridSearchCV across 6 models — Decision Tree, Bagging, Random Forest, AdaBoost, GBM, XGBoost
  • Step 3: joblib serialization of best pipeline to .pkl
  • Step 4: Flask REST API — POST /v1/predict endpoint with JSON input/output
  • Step 5: Docker container for Flask API → deployed to HuggingFace Docker Space
  • Step 6: Streamlit frontend UI → deployed to HuggingFace Streamlit Space

Conclusions & Recommendations

  • API-driven forecasting enables supply chain and procurement teams to get per-product predictions without data science involvement
  • Tier 1 / Type 1 stores can support higher-MRP products; Tier 3 stores should focus on high-volume, lower-MRP staples
  • Increase display area allocation for snack foods and fruits/vegetables — highest-revenue categories consistently
  • This project demonstrates the full MLOps loop — from raw data to a publicly accessible REST API

Medical RAG Assistant

RAG-based Q&A system over the 4,114-page Merck Manual using LangChain + ChromaDB + Mistral-7B. Baseline LLM hallucinated on all 5 clinical queries. RAG achieved perfect Groundedness (5/5) and high Relevance (4–5/5) across all queries.

Groundedness5/5 all queries
Relevance4–5/5 all queries
Chunks indexed4,692 passages
▼

Business Problem

  • The Merck Manual spans 4,114 pages across 23 clinical sections — clinicians cannot search it quickly during patient care
  • Baseline LLMs truncate responses at 128 tokens and hallucinate citations — clinically dangerous
  • Goal: Build a grounded Q&A system that answers medical queries with real citations from the Merck Manual, zero hallucinations

RAG Pipeline Architecture

  • Ingestion: PyMuPDFLoader → 4,114-page PDF → RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter (chunk_overlap=200) → 4,692 chunks
  • Embeddings: SentenceTransformerEmbeddings (general-purpose; identified as upgrade opportunity → BioBERT)
  • Vector store: ChromaDB (persistent) with k=3 retrieval (identified as too restrictive for multi-topic queries)
  • LLM: Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 (Q6_K quantized, llama.cpp) · max_tokens=128 · temperature=0
  • Chain: LangChain RetrievalQA with custom medical prompt template

Experiment Results

QueryGroundednessRelevance
Sepsis management5/54/5
Appendicitis5/54/5
Alopecia areata5/55/5
Brain injury (TBI)5/54/5
Wilderness leg fracture5/54/5
  • Baseline LLM: hallucinated citations, truncated at 128 tokens, zero groundedness
  • Prompt engineering alone: marginal improvement, hallucination persisted

Conclusions & Recommendations

  • Immediate: Switch to BioBERT embeddings (+20% estimated retrieval accuracy); increase max_tokens to 256–512; increase k from 3 → 5–7
  • Short-term: Expand eval to 100-query expert-validated test set; add confidence scoring to flag uncertain responses; hybrid search (semantic + keyword)
  • Long-term: Pilot with 10–20 clinicians; multi-source KB integration; HIPAA/GDPR-compliant data handling
  • Add "This is not medical advice — consult a physician" disclaimer for any patient-facing deployment
  • Monetization: SaaS for clinics/hospitals; API licensing for EMR software vendors
